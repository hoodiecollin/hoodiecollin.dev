There's a recurring temptation to reach for a server, a database, and a CMS for
a personal site. For most of them, it's the wrong default.

## What static buys you

A static export is just files. That has real consequences:

1. **It can't go down the way a server can.** There's nothing to keep running.
2. **It's cheap to host** — a CDN or object store is enough.
3. **It's fast by construction** — every page is prerendered.

## What you give up

Interactivity, mostly — and even that's overstated. Client components handle the
dynamic parts (theme, search), and the content itself rarely needs a runtime.

> The content is the point. The infrastructure should get out of its way.

For a writing-first site, MDX-at-build-time hits the sweet spot: real components
when you want them, plain prose when you don't.
